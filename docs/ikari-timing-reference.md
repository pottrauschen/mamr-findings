# Ikari Warriors Timing Reference: Hardware, MiSTer, MAME and MAMR

This document compares the timing of the three Z80 CPUs of SNK Ikari
Warriors (1986) on the original board, in the MiSTer FPGA core, in MAME 0.289
and in MAMR. For every timing rule it states where the rule comes from. Neither
MAME nor MiSTer is treated as ground truth. It extends
[docs/hardware-spec.md](hardware-spec.md) (sections 5, 6, 8 and open items 3
and 8) and the section "Verification against MAME" of MAMR's own README (not
published); facts documented
there are linked, not repeated. Sections 1 to 12 describe MAMR at the
revision in the table below; section 13 records the change made after
experiment E1, section 14 experiments E2 and E3 and the change made after
E3, section 15 the frame lockstep against MAME 0.285 with a reproduction
of MAME's scheduler.

## 1. Executive Summary

- Very little of the original board's timing is measured. The schematic
  shows that CPU A and CPU B share one 3.35 MHz clock derived from the video
  counter, that the sound CPU has its own 8 MHz / 2 clock, that both main
  CPUs get their VBlank INT from the same VBL edge, and that a PAL-driven
  arbiter gives CPU A priority on the shared video bus and puts CPU B into
  WAIT (SCHEMATIC, PAL/JED) [SCH p.3 H2 7L] [SCH p.1 E2-3 oscillator]
  [SCH p.3 D-E 3 7N] [SCH p.3 G2-3 CPU B] [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:4].
  Frame length, VBL position and wait-state counts are not measured
  (section 3, section 12).
- MiSTer reproduces this structure from the schematic and a PAL dump, but
  replaces CPU B's WAIT by clock gating, needs a HACK register for a stable
  line, and gives 416 x 262 at 61.47 Hz with 54,496 CPU cycles per frame
  [M rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:341]
  [M rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:136] [MEAS-1].
- MAME uses 60 Hz without raw timing, no arbitration, no wait states and a
  1/6000 s slice scheme; it is a functional model, not ground truth
  [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4254; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4259].
- MAMR copies MAME's constants and slice scheme. It differs from MAME and
  from the Z80 chip in interrupt sampling (between blocks), the EI delay
  (rest of block plus one unit, also after RETI/RETN) and bus access timing
  (instruction start) [Z80UM p.26] [Z80UM p.32]
  [MAMR runtime/z80_cpu.cpp:76; runtime/z80_cpu.cpp:111; recompiler/emit_c.cpp:466].
  It differs from the board in A/B phase, CPU B wait states and cross-CPU
  ordering, where the schematic shows a common clock and an arbiter
  [SCH p.3 H2 7L] [SCH p.8 F-G 5-6].
- The replay divergence against MAME (permanent from frame 7989) is a timing
  race of CPU A's main program against the VBlank IRQ. It traces back to
  MAMR taking CPU B's pending IRQ only after the whole block containing EI
  at 0x0097 [MEAS-7] [MEAS-9]. The R register is an amplifier, not the cause:
  injecting MAME's R values only delays the onset to 9632, and interpreter
  mode delays it to 9773 [MEAS-10].
- At that revision no MAMR timing change was recommended before experiments
  E1 and E2 (section 11, Decision). E1 has since been run and the change
  made (section 13): MAMR takes interrupts between instructions with the
  Zilog EI rule, and the permanent replay divergence moved from frame 7989
  to 9773 [MEAS-11] [MEAS-12].

## 2. What We Know for Certain

Each item below is backed by a primary source, by code at a fixed revision or
by a reproducible emulator run.

1. CPU A and CPU B share one clock net. It is the video counter bit H0
   (13.4 MHz / 4 = 3.35 MHz), buffered by LS32 7L with one input grounded
   (SCHEMATIC) [SCH p.3 H2 7L] [SCH p.3 D2 CPU A] [SCH p.3 G2 CPU B]
   [SCH p.8 G6-7 H counter].
2. The sound CPU has its own 8 MHz oscillator divided by two, so it runs at
   4 MHz asynchronous to the 13.4 MHz timebase (SCHEMATIC)
   [SCH p.1 E2-3 oscillator] [SCH p.1 D2 MUSIC CPU].
3. CPU B's WAIT pin is driven by BWA from the arbiter on the clock board;
   CPU A's WAIT and BUSRQ, CPU B's BUSRQ and the sound CPU's WAIT, NMI and
   BUSRQ have no driving logic (SCHEMATIC) [SCH p.3 G2-3 CPU B]
   [SCH p.3 C-D 2 CPU A] [SCH p.1 C-D 2 MUSIC CPU].
4. The VBlank INT of CPU A and CPU B comes from two halves of an LS74 (7N),
   both clocked by the same VBL edge and each cleared by its own CPU's IORQ
   (SCHEMATIC) [SCH p.3 D-E 3 7N] [SCH p.3 G-H 3 7N].
5. CPU A and CPU B reach the shared video bus through one multiplexed address
   bus (three LS157), read latches (LS373, enabled by RLA/RLB) and write
   drivers (LS244, enabled by WBA/WBB) (SCHEMATIC) [SCH p.3 E-F 7-8]
   [SCH p.3 E9 4N] [SCH p.3 F9 8N] [SCH p.3 E-F 9 4P/8P].
6. The arbiter samples the requests AE and BE in LS107 JK flip-flops clocked
   once per H0 period and sequences the bus with PAL16R6 A5004-1 and an LS194
   (SCHEMATIC) [SCH p.8 F-G 5-6] [SCH p.8 G6 6A] [SCH p.8 F-G 4 6D]. The
   MiSTer model of that PAL is converted from a JED dump (PAL/JED)
   [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:4].
7. On a real Z80, INT is sampled at the end of each instruction, EI delays
   acceptance by exactly one following instruction, NMI is edge-triggered,
   and R counts M1 cycles in bits 0-6 (SCHEMATIC, manufacturer manual)
   [Z80UM p.26] [Z80UM p.32] [Z80UM p.20] [Z80UM p.17]. Prefixed opcodes
   add 2 to R, accepted interrupts add 1, and EI or DD/FD chains block INT
   (MEASURED by Young on real machines) [YOUNG 6.1, p.25] [YOUNG 5, p.21]
   [YOUNG 5.5, p.23-24].
8. MAME 0.289 runs ikari at 3.35/3.35/4 MHz, a 60 Hz screen, a 1/6000 s
   maximum quantum, VBlank `irq0_line_hold` on CPU A and B and plain shared
   RAM without wait states [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4243; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4259; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4254; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4245; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:1574].
9. MAMR copies these MAME constants and the MAME slice scheme
   [MAMR games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:12-13; games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:349-355].
10. In a replay of a recorded MAME game, MAMR's shared RAM outside the stacks
    stays identical to MAME's until frame 7988 except in transient episodes and
    differs permanently from frame 7989 [MEAS-7]. The frame-7989 onset is a
    timing race, not an R value difference [MEAS-9] [MEAS-10].

## 3. Original Hardware and Measurement Data

Very little about the original board's timing is measured. The primary
sources are one scanned schematic with the MiSTer core author's annotations,
one PAL dump, the Zilog manual and third-party Z80 chip tests. No source
measures the frame rate, the line count, the VBlank position, the phase of
the CPU clock against the video counter, CPU B wait states or NMI latency on
an Ikari board.

Sources and their limits:

- The schematic is a scan with annotations added by the core author
  (for example "Fix: CK1n" and "LS10 -> 3-input NAND gate"), which are not
  SNK content [SCH p.2 text layer] [SCH p.8 text layer]. Some overbars and
  inverter designators are not legible [SCH p.8 G6 6A] [SCH p.3 F5-6].
- PAL A5004-1 (arbiter sequencer) exists as a JED dump converted by the
  MiSTer author (PAL/JED) [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:4-5]. Three of its terms are
  marked FIXED, one of them "infered from logic analyzer capture"
  [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:93; rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:100; rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:120].
  The same comment and term appear in the TripleZ80 PAL, which is converted
  from an Armored Scrum Object JED, so the capture may not come from an Ikari
  board [T3 rtl/clocks/pal16r6_15B_sync.v:3; rtl/clocks/pal16r6_15B_sync.v:122].
- PAL A5004-2 (video chip-select decoder) is only described as "Derived
  from" the PAL; no dump is cited (UNKNOWN) [M rtl/cpuA_B/A5004_2.v:3].
- Two MiSTer comments give gate delays as measured (25 ns, 7.5 ns) without
  saying who measured them on which board (MEASURED, unattributed)
  [M rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:378; rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:493].
- MAME marks the three CPU clocks "verified on pcb" without data (MAME)
  [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4243; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4247; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4251].

## 4. MiSTer Model

The core runs everything on one 53.6 MHz clock (4 x 13.4 MHz) with clock
enables and edge-detected pseudo-clocks (IMPLEMENTATION)
[M rtl/pll/pll_0002.v:28] [M Arcade-IkariWarriors.sv:382]
[M rtl/ttl/DFF_pseudoAsyncClrPre.v:37]. The clock and video logic was adapted
from the author's Alpha Mission core, so RTL designators (E9, F15, G15, E6,
E4, C1) do not match the schematic (7X, 5C, 5A, 6D, 7N, 3L)
[M rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:1] [M Arcade-IkariWarriors.sv:366].

What it reproduces from the schematic or the PAL dump:

- H/V counters, VCK, VBL flip-flop and the INT flip-flops set by VBL and
  cleared by IORQ [M rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:258; rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:313]
  [M rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:127-128; rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:330].
- The arbiter (AE/BE, request flip-flops, PAL A5004-1, BWA, RLA/RLB, WBA/WBB)
  [M rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:378; rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:603-606].
- The NMI flip-flops, set at the end of the other CPU's read strobe and
  cleared during the own write [M rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:453-454; rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:484-485].

Resulting timing (MEASURED-EMU of the RTL model): 416 x 262 at 6.7 MHz,
61.4724 Hz, 54,496 CPU A/B cycles per frame, VBL at V=0xFA [MEAS-1].

The Z80 is T80 version 350 in the T80pa wrapper for all three CPUs
[M rtl/T80/T80.vhd:5] [M rtl/T80/T80pa.vhd:70]. T80 accepts INT and NMI only at
the last T-state of an instruction, never after a prefix and never at the end
of EI [M rtl/T80/T80.vhd:1303]; it increments R(6:0) in every M1 including
prefixes, HALT and interrupt acknowledge [M rtl/T80/T80.vhd:481-488]. The
TripleZ80 core uses the same arbiter equations but connects BWA to CPU B's
real WAIT_n [T3 rtl/cpuA_B/AlphaMissionCore_CPU_A_B_sync.sv:325-328].

## 5. MAME Model

MAME 0.289's ikari driver is a functional model; it has no raster timing,
no bus arbitration and no wait states.

- Clocks 3.35/3.35/4 MHz; screen 60 Hz, 288 x 224 with visible lines
  8-223, no raw timing [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4243-4251; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4258-4260].
  The derived budget is 55,833.33 CPU A/B cycles per frame and a VBlank of
  8 lines (595 us) [MAME src/emu/screen.cpp:1002; src/emu/screen.cpp:1010].
- Scheduling: maximum quantum 1/6000 s (558.33 CPU A/B cycles); in each
  slice CPU A, then CPU B, then the sound CPU run to the slice target
  [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4254; src/emu/schedule.cpp:409; src/emu/schedule.cpp:820].
- VBlank: `irq0_line_hold` on CPU A and CPU B at VBlank start; the line is
  cleared on interrupt acknowledge [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4245; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4249; src/emu/diexec.cpp:784-788].
- NMI handshake: CPU A reading 0xC700 asserts B's NMI, B writing 0xC000
  clears it; the reverse for A [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:1588; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:1603].
  Line changes go through `scheduler().synchronize()`, which aborts the
  running slice [MAME src/emu/diexec.cpp:691; src/emu/schedule.cpp:99-100].
- Shared RAM 0xD000-0xFFFF is plain RAM in both CPU maps
  [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:1573-1575]. WAIT and BUSRQ are not connected;
  the Z80 core would honour WAIT only per timeslice
  [MAME src/devices/cpu/z80/z80make.py:295-297].
- Z80: interrupts are checked before every instruction; only `ei()` sets
  the one-instruction shadow; RETI/RETN copy IFF2 to IFF1 without it; NMI is
  latched on the rising edge; R is incremented per opcode fetch and per
  interrupt entry; a slice may end inside an instruction
  [MAME src/devices/cpu/z80/z80.lst:1021; src/devices/cpu/z80/z80.cpp:612; src/devices/cpu/z80/z80.lst:184; src/devices/cpu/z80/z80.cpp:799; src/devices/cpu/z80/z80.lst:54; src/devices/cpu/z80/z80.lst:897; src/devices/cpu/z80/z80make.py:115].

Derived consequence of the slice order (IMPLEMENTATION, reasoning, not
measured): when A triggers B, B catches up to A's time; when B triggers A,
A has already run to the slice end and reacts up to one quantum late; B sees
A's writes of the current slice even at earlier B times
[MAME src/emu/schedule.cpp:490] [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:743; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:755].
These are scheduling artefacts, not hardware properties.

All four count R by the same rule (MATCH). The parity quirk does not matter
for Ikari: the site at 0x52DC is followed by AND 0x03, which overwrites the
flags [MAMR generated/ikari/maincpu_blocks.cpp:68543-68546]. Only CPU A reads R,
at nine sites [MAMR generated/ikari/maincpu_blocks.cpp:35722; generated/ikari/maincpu_blocks.cpp:76734].

Can R alone explain the divergence? No.

- R differs because the instruction streams differ, not because the
  counting rule differs: the first LD A,R mismatch (frame 1483) coincides
  with one wait-loop pass less in MAMR and a 19-cycle shift [MEAS-8].
- 92 of 102 LD A,R values differ before the onset, but only 14 after the
  game's masking, and those cause only transient RAM episodes [MEAS-8] [MEAS-7].
- Injecting MAME's R values moves the permanent onset from 7989 to 9632 but
  does not remove it [MEAS-10]. The frame-7989 onset itself is a race of
  CPU A's main program against the VBlank IRQ, not an R value [MEAS-9].

R is an amplifier: it turns cycle-level differences in how many
instructions run between two events into different random numbers. On the
board R would also depend on the real frame length and on any timing
difference between board and emulator, so neither MAME nor MAMR can predict
the board's R values (UNKNOWN).

How A and B are synchronised on shared memory:

- Board: both CPUs run on one clock [SCH p.3 H2 7L]. Requests are sampled
  once per H0 period [SCH p.8 G6 6A]. CPU A has priority and CPU B is held in
  WAIT when both request and A owns the bus (SCHEMATIC, PAL/JED)
  [SCH p.3 G2-3 CPU B] [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:106]. CPU A has no wait input
  [SCH p.3 C-D 2 CPU A], so its read must complete through the latch within
  its own cycle; whether the PAL slot sequence guarantees this for every
  alignment is UNKNOWN. A real Z80 samples WAIT on the falling edge of T2
  and of each wait state [Z80UM p.21].
- MiSTer: same equations; CPU B is stopped by masking its clock enable,
  which can freeze it in any half T-state instead of inserting wait states
  (FPGA_ADAPTATION) [M rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:341]
  [M rtl/T80/T80pa.vhd:209]. TripleZ80 uses the real WAIT_n
  [T3 rtl/cpuA_B/AlphaMissionCore_CPU_A_B_sync.sv:328]. Video RAM is
  dual-ported, so only CPU-versus-CPU contention is modelled
  [M rtl/back1/IkariWarriorsCore_Back1.sv:184].
- MAME: no arbitration; both CPUs access the same plain RAM at their own
  local times, ordered only by the scheduler [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:1573-1575]
  [MAME src/emu/schedule.cpp:409].
- MAMR: no arbitration; one plain array; ordering by slices, blocks and the
  three catch-up points [MAMR games/ikari/ikari_machine.h:38]
  [MAMR games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:29].

The game synchronises A and B through flags in this RAM: CPU A polls CPU B's
done flag FEC0 once per frame at 0x24D5-0x24DD
[MAMR generated/ikari/maincpu_blocks.cpp:26814-26832], and the frame-7989
race involves that poll [MEAS-9]. On the board such a flag write and poll are
ordered in real time on one bus, and CPU B's write may be delayed by wait
states; no emulator except MiSTer models these delays (SCHEMATIC for the
board side) [SCH p.3 G2-3 CPU B].

## 10. Most Likely Cause of the Replay Divergence

The divergence is MAMR against MAME. None of these hypotheses says which of
the two is closer to the board.

### H1. Block-granular EI shadow on CPU B (likelihood high)

- Mechanism: CPU B executes EI at 0x0097 while its VBlank IRQ is pending.
  MAMR finishes the whole block (which writes the flag FEC0) and one more
  unit before the IRQ; MAME takes the IRQ after one instruction. B's FEC0
  write moves about 8077 CPU-A cycles earlier from frame 7959. In frames
  7981/7982 CPU A's FEC0 poll takes the other path (30 T vs 11 T), so A's
  main program runs 19 cycles early, and in frame 7989 its FDD0 test at
  0x35BA lands before the VBlank IRQ instead of after it.
- Evidence: [MEAS-9] [MEAS-10]; the shadow rule
  [MAMR runtime/z80_cpu.cpp:111]; EI does not end a block
  [MAMR recompiler/emit_c.cpp:451]; the block
  [MAMR generated/ikari/sub_blocks.cpp:253-277]. Zilog specifies a delay of
  exactly one instruction (SCHEMATIC) [Z80UM p.32] [Z80UM p.197].
- Affected: `runtime/z80_cpu.cpp` `z80_cpu_run_until` (shadow arming),
  `recompiler/emit_c.cpp` (block termination).
- Experiment: build outside the repository that ends a block after the
  instruction following EI and then arms no further shadow; expect the
  onset to move from 7989 to about 9773, as in interpreter mode.
- Effort: small.
- Result: E1 removes the 7989 onset but reaches only 8003; 9773 needs
  interrupt sampling between instructions (section 13) [MEAS-11] [MEAS-12].

### H2. Residual intra-instruction timing (likelihood medium)

- Mechanism: with one instruction per unit and either EI rule, the replay
  still diverges at 9773 with the same 19-cycle FEC0 signature. Remaining
  candidates are bus accesses timed at instruction start, overshoot of the

### H3. Cross-CPU synchronisation model (likelihood medium)

- Mechanism: MAMR catches up only the addressed CPU, only forward and only
  at three addresses; MAME's `synchronize()` cuts the running slice for all
  CPUs. When B triggers A's NMI, A has already run ahead. The sound command
  sets status bits immediately in MAMR and through a timer in MAME.
- Evidence: [MAMR games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:29; games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:151; games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:169; games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:195]
  [MAME src/emu/diexec.cpp:691; src/emu/schedule.cpp:99-100; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:913]
  [MAMR README.md:127-131]. Not measured in isolation.
- Affected: `games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp` (`catch_up`, the 0xC000, 0xC400
  and 0xC700 handlers).
- Experiment: harness variant that ends the slice of every CPU on a line
  change, combined with H1, and compare onsets.
- Effort: medium.

### H4. R register values (likelihood low as cause, high as amplifier)

- Mechanism: different R values change enemy decisions.
- Evidence: R differs only because instruction counts differ; injecting
  MAME's values delays but does not remove the divergence; the 7989 onset
  is not R-driven [MEAS-8] [MEAS-9] [MEAS-10].
- Affected: none in the R rule itself [MAMR recompiler/emit_c.cpp:467].
- Experiment: done (R injection) [MEAS-10].
- Effort: small.

## 13. Follow-up: E1 and Instruction-Granular Interrupts

E1 was run outside the repository on MAMR13P; MAMR was changed afterwards.
Citations `MAMR13` refer to the revision that adds this section; sections 1
to 12 still describe revision MAMR.

What changed:

- Analyzer: the address after the instruction that follows an EI is a
  leader (unless that instruction ends the block anyway or the code range
  ends there), so a block containing EI ends at the latest where the Z80
  first accepts INT after it [Z80UM p.32]; where the next instruction is a
  leader already, the block ends with the EI itself (CPU A 0x009E, sound
  0x009F). The stack-idiom simulation continues across these extra block
  ends [MAMR13 recompiler/cfg.cpp:61-80; recompiler/cfg.cpp:175-189;
  recompiler/cfg.cpp:273-283; recompiler/cfg.cpp:381-382]. In Ikari this
  adds three blocks (CPU A 0x00A1, CPU B 0x009B, sound 0x00A1; 2050
  instead of 2047).
- Emitter: after each instruction of a block except the last and an
  unconditional exit, the block compares its T-states with a time budget;
  when the budget is used up, it stores the next PC and returns. An entry
  switch continues at that instruction in a later call (resume point). A
  block that returns right after EI, at such a stop or because the EI is
  its last instruction, sets `after_ei`
  [MAMR13 recompiler/emit_c.cpp:661-711]. Every instruction of a block is
  registered as an entry of its function
  [MAMR13 games/ikari/ikari_generated.h:16-26].
- Runner: the budget is the time left to the run target, so a CPU runs past
  its target by at most one instruction and an interrupt raised at the
  target is taken between the same two instructions as on the chip
  [Z80UM p.26]. An NMI raised while the CPU runs a block (from a bus
  callback) sets the budget to 0, and so does an INT raised that way while
  INT is enabled (`z80_cpu_set_int`). A unit that ends right after EI (an
  interpreted EI or `after_ei`) holds INT back for one instruction: the
  next block gets the budget 0 and stops after its first instruction. NMI
  is not held back, and RETI/RETN no longer delay INT [Z80UM p.32]
  [MAMR13 runtime/z80_cpu.cpp:55-71; runtime/z80_cpu.cpp:102-139].

In section 7 this turns rows 8 and 9 into MATCH for MAMR against MAME,
MiSTer and the hardware rule, and in row 16 block atomicity becomes
instruction atomicity: a CPU that is caught up stops at the first
instruction boundary at or past the time of the access. Rows 15 and 18 are
unchanged.

Interpretation of E2: the 9773 onset is not a cycle count inside an
instruction (H2) but the order in which CPU B sees CPU A's writes (H3).
MAME and MAMR both let one CPU see the other CPU's writes from up to one
slice in its future, because a CPU runs to the end of its slice before
the next one starts [MAME src/emu/schedule.cpp:409]
[MAMR games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:349-355]; they differ because the
slices end at different times. Neither order is the board's, where both
CPUs work on one bus at the same time and CPU B waits while CPU A owns it
(SCHEMATIC, PAL/JED; section 9) [SCH p.3 G2-3 CPU B]
[M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:106].

What changed (E3): before every access of CPU A or CPU B to the memory
both see (shared RAM 0xD000-0xFFFF, the hard-flag registers and results
0xCC00-0xCEE0), the other main CPU is brought up to the time of the access
[MAMR14 games/ikari/ikari_machine.cpp:91-147]. A CPU that is caught up
stops at the first instruction boundary at or past that time (section 13),
and catching up only runs forward. CPU A runs first in each slice and
brings CPU B along before every shared access; when CPU B runs on to the
end of the slice afterwards, CPU A is already there. Each CPU therefore
sees the other's shared accesses in time order, to the instruction: still
timed at the start of the instruction, with no wait states for CPU B and
no slot timing of the arbiter, which stay UNKNOWN (section 12). In row 16
of section 7 MAMR's catch-up becomes "every shared access" instead of
three addresses; the catch-ups before line changes are unchanged.

Consequences [MEAS-14]:

- In frame 9768 CPU B now reads the old sprite 30 (0x1F, as MAMR before,
  but now in time order). The onset against MAME stays at 9773, and MAMR
  differs from MAME a little more often before it, because MAME keeps its
  slice order.
- Power-on tests, frames 211-301: CPU A clears the background VRAM with
  `LDIR` at 0x1A44-0x1A4F (0xD800-0xDFFF filled with 0xFF) while CPU B
  copies a test picture into it at 0x013A-0x0164; both reach the same
  addresses at about the same time. In MAME CPU B's picture survives, in
  time order CPU A's fill does, so MAMR shows a plain background for these
  91 frames where MAME shows the picture. Which one the board shows is
  UNKNOWN; with CPU A's bus priority the board's order is closer to time
  order than to MAME's slice order, but this is not measured.
- Results no longer depend on where the 1/6000 s slices end.

Interpretation: E2 answers the question of section 11 (cycle source of the
9773 onset); E3 removes the slice order from MAMR's model. Matching MAME
past 9773 would mean copying MAME's slice ends, which are a scheduling
artefact (section 5), so it is not a goal. Still open: CPU B's wait states
and the arbiter slots (E5, E6), bus accesses at their machine cycle instead
of the start of the instruction, and the separate effect of the RETI/RETN
rule.

## Decision

```text
MISTER_USEFUL_AS_REFERENCE = PARTLY
MAME_GROUND_TRUTH = NO
HARDWARE_TIMING_UNDERSTOOD = PARTLY
MAMR_TIMING_CHANGE_RECOMMENDED_NOW = NO (at revision MAMR; made since, section 13)
```

- MISTER_USEFUL_AS_REFERENCE = PARTLY: its structure follows the schematic
  and a JED dump (clocks, INT, NMI, arbiter) [SCH p.8 F-G 5-6]
  [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:4], but CPU B's wait is clock gating, the H
  counter needs a HACK, three PAL terms are hand-fixed and nothing in it is
  measured against a board [M rtl/cpuA_B/IkariWarriorsCore_CPU_A_B.sv:341]
  [M rtl/clocks/IkariWarriorsCore_Clocks.sv:136] [M rtl/clocks/A5004_1.v:120].
- MAME_GROUND_TRUTH = NO: 60 Hz without raw timing, no arbitration, no wait
  states and slice-ordered visibility are model choices
  [MAME src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4254; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:4259; src/mame/snk/snk.cpp:1573-1575].
- HARDWARE_TIMING_UNDERSTOOD = PARTLY: clocks, INT, NMI structure, WAIT
  wiring and arbiter topology are on the schematic; frame length, VBL
  position, wait-state counts and NMI decode are not measured or not legible
  (section 12) [SCH p.3 H2 7L] [SCH p.8 G6 6A].
- MAMR_TIMING_CHANGE_RECOMMENDED_NOW = NO: the only rule with primary chip
  evidence that MAMR violates and that the measurements tie to the
  divergence is the EI delay (Zilog: exactly one instruction) [Z80UM p.32]
  [MEAS-9]. Fixing it is not one small change: it needs a block end after
  the instruction following EI plus a different shadow rule in
  `runtime/z80_cpu.cpp`, and MAMR would still sample interrupts only between
  blocks, which also differs from the chip [Z80UM p.26]
  [MAMR runtime/z80_cpu.cpp:76]. Its measured benefit is only closeness to
  MAME [MEAS-10]. Run E1 first; decide after E1 and E2.
- Update after E1: the change was made (section 13). It implements two
  rules of the Zilog manual, interrupt sampling at the end of every
  instruction and the one-instruction EI delay [Z80UM p.26] [Z80UM p.32],
  so it rests on chip evidence, not only on closeness to MAME. E2 was not
  needed for that decision; it stays open for the 9773 onset [MEAS-12].
- Update after E2 and E3 (section 14): the 9773 onset is the order in which
  CPU B sees CPU A's writes, not a cycle count [MEAS-13]. MAMR now orders
  shared accesses of CPU A and B in time, as the board's one shared bus
  does (SCHEMATIC) [SCH p.3 G2-3 CPU B]; this moves MAMR no closer to MAME
  and changes 91 frames of the power-on test screen [MEAS-14].
- Update after the frame lockstep (section 15): MAMR's chips under MAME's
  scheduler are identical to MAME 0.285 in every field over 3000 attract
  frames and a 112,000-frame game [MEAS-15]; the game build's differences
  are scheduling, and under cycle interleaving MAME would show MAMR's
  power-on test screen [MEAS-16]. No timing change of the game build is
  made; bus accesses at their machine cycle in stage S stay open.
